Coherence & Character

The Monday Question

November 18, 2026

On Thursday, Trump, Xi, and Putin will meet in Shenzhen. Nuclear arms are on the agenda, but American officials don’t expect an agreement.

I suspect much of the meeting will be theater, and I mean that somewhat dismissively. Still, theater has its uses. A staged handshake can mean something, maybe even get people talking who otherwise wouldn’t. It’s irritating to give such pageantry attention, but there it is.

I’ve worked with leaders who are very skilled at rallying people to their cause. They can engage with people who have little in common and come away with everyone feeling a sense of progress. It comes naturally to them, it’s a real skill, and it often leads to good results. I’ve also seen people leave those meetings feeling good without really knowing whether anything had changed.

The leader can mean every word and be just as convinced as everyone else that something was accomplished.

We tend to treat authenticity as meaning you believe what you’re saying. I think it’s something else: whether there’s anything real underneath what you’re saying. A leader can be completely sincere and still be fooling themselves.

Some of it will be true. Three men who don’t usually share a stage really will be sharing one, which is its own kind of arms control. But being on the same stage doesn’t mean they’ve agreed to do anything. People rally behind moments like that because they feel like the right direction, and it can be a long time before anyone asks how we’re supposed to get there.

What I expect from Shenzhen is mostly hope that something will come of it. I suppose that’s enough to keep us transfixed. These three are good at commanding attention, and they’ll have plenty of it. Then everyone will fly home, and I’ll ask the question I ask after any meeting that felt good: what is anyone doing differently on Monday?
