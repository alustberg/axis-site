Not a Score. A Shape.

Most leadership assessments give you a number and then disappear. The Axis Diamond isn't a score. It's a shape — and it changes the question you're able to ask.

Most leaders have taken some kind of leadership assessment before. Probably more than one. You answered the questions, got the score, read the summary. You had a moment with it. Compared notes with someone. Tried to act on a piece of it here and there. And then it just sort of… disappeared.

The Axis Diamond isn't a score. It's a shape.

When you plot it on the Leadership Axis, the shape is right there. It makes an elegant spider chart — which frankly delights me every time. But the diamond is more than the chart.

A raw diamond already has its structure — the crystal lattice formed under pressure, over time. You don't create that structure. You reveal it. Cutting a diamond isn't about reshaping it into something new. It's about finding the angles that let the light in — and every stone catches it differently.

Unlike a list of strengths and gaps that implies hierarchy, a shape implies configuration — everything is present, but distributed differently.

The Diamond maps four dimensions. Strategy and Authenticity sit on the vertical axis — direction and ground. Where you're headed and what you actually believe. Systems and Humanity sit on the horizontal — structure and connection. How things work and how people experience them.

Every leader forms a unique shape across these four. And that shape is neither good nor bad. It's not aspirational. It's not a report card.

The Diamond doesn't tell a leader what to build. It shows them where they already stand. And once that's visible, a different question becomes possible. Not "what am I missing?" but "what's already here that I haven't been using?"

Every stone catches the light differently. That's not a flaw. That's the whole point.
